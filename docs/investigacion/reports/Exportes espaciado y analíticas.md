# Expose stored data before building new metrics

Compared with leading rostering products, the app has few gaps left, and most of them are about showing data it already stores rather than computing new metrics. Commit `836b510` on branch `exportes-calidad` (not yet merged when this was written) already brings the Excel and print outputs up to vendor and government practice. Every workbook now prints A4 landscape at one page wide, repeats its titles on each page, has filters, real dates and percentages, and carries a title, generation time and page numbers in its margins. The commit also adds an "Alertas" sheet to the roster workbook and a one-click zip of the whole period. The printed roster now shows the area, the version or "BORRADOR", the print date, the legend and a signature line. Five items should come next. The first is **missing staff-hours per position**: the coverage figure sums all positions in each slot, so a surplus in one position hides a shortfall in another. The second is **a fatigue threshold sized for 12-hour shifts**, because the streak signal fires at more than six days in a row whatever the shift length. The third is **per-person change notices with explicit dates**, built from the existing Original/Cambios diff. The fourth is **a daily sign-in sheet**. The fifth is **the change-log exceptions and their reasons in the Alertas sheet**, plus the roster version and the overnight-hours rule written into each workbook. A weekly overtime-cap alert, an hours-bank tolerance band and a period scorecard come after those. The scorecard should keep its parts as separate traffic lights rather than merging them into one weighted number. The payroll export should wait until the payroll system is known, then be one fixed template for that system. A workload ratio needs monthly production counts the service would have to supply. Spacing needs nothing beyond PR 71. The evidence for all of this is vendor documentation, a UK government spreadsheet standard and user reviews. No study ties an export format or layout to better rostering decisions.

## Nine analytics PRs and one export commit cover the vendor baseline

The build order from the earlier metrics research is complete. PRs #62 to #70 added coverage per 30-minute slot with a weekday × hour map, stability after publication with publication lead time, fatigue signals, absence patterns and the Bradford factor, deviation from the team mean, overtime concentration, request fulfilment, an absence forecast, solver-run history, the next Sunday-surcharge step, and notice and replacement rates for absences. The dated rates are in place. `TARIFAS_BASE` in `an/turnos/db.py` starts the night window at 19:00 from 25 December 2025, and sets the Sunday/holiday surcharge to 90% from 1 July 2026 and 100% from 1 July 2027. These match the Ley 2466 steps reported by secondary sources ([Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/latinoamerica/colombia/pago-de-domingos-y-festivos-sube-al-90-desde-hoy-1-de-julio-de-2026-en-colombia/); [Actualícese](https://actualicese.com/recargo-nocturno-en-colombia-conoce-el-horario-aplicable-a-partir-del-25-de-diciembre-de-2025/)).

The export set matches what vendors ship. When I Work's schedule export has two tabs: the shift list, and total hours and wages per person ([When I Work](https://help.wheniwork.com/articles/exporting-schedules)). Planday adds working-time-rule breaches, contracted vs scheduled hours and a schedule history log ([Planday](https://help.planday.com/en/articles/30408-generate-reports-in-planday)). The app's roster workbook holds the current roster, the published original, a sheet of differences and hour formulas, and after `836b510` the open alerts. The analytics workbook now has 17 sheets.

Commit `836b510` closes most of the output gaps that user reviews complain about. QGenda reviewers fault its audit trail, printing and report setup ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/90628/QGenda/reviews/)). When I Work reviewers want to print directly and to get several schedules in one document ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/121248/When-I-Work/reviews/)). Deputy reviewers want control over font size and bold in printed exports ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/deputy/reviews_and_filters)). Humanity reviewers say its reports cover "the basics" but miss some fields ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/humanity-schedule-by-tcp/reviews)). The period zip answers the request for several schedules in one document. A dedicated roster print layout answers the printing complaint, and the Alertas sheet answers part of the audit-trail complaint. What remains is small additions to outputs that already exist: version and date rules in the workbooks, exception reasons, change notices and a daily sheet. Two problems in the analytics code also turned up and are covered below.

## Workbooks now meet the spreadsheet standard; two labels and one payroll decision remain

The strongest primary source is the UK Government Analysis Function's standard for releasing statistics in spreadsheets. It starts each workbook with a cover sheet: **title in A1**, then a summary, the source, the date, whether the figures are provisional, and a contact. A contents sheet and a single notes table follow. Each sheet holds **one table**, with unique headers and no merged cells or blank rows inside it. Units go in brackets, and colour is never the only signal ([Analysis Function](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/releasing-statistics-in-spreadsheets/)). Microsoft's page-setup guidance covers printing: **repeat header rows and the name column on every page, use landscape, and fit to one page wide with automatic height** ([Microsoft](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/Excel/page-setup)). Deputy pre-formats its Excel export for the same reason ([Deputy](https://www.deputy.com/blog/schedule-printing-improved)).

`836b510` does nearly all of this. A new `_terminar` step runs on every workbook. It sets A4 landscape at one page wide, takes the repeated print rows and columns from the frozen panes, and writes the title, sheet name, generation time and "Página x de y" into the header and footer. `_tabla` adds a filter to each table sheet. `_valor` writes dates as dates and percentages as fractions formatted `0.0%`, and leaves a cell empty where it used to print "—". The analytics summary ends with the date range and the generation time. The UK guidance advises against freeze panes and filters for accessibility reasons ([Analysis Function checklist](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/making-spreadsheets-accessible-a-brief-checklist-of-the-basics/)), but it targets published statistics read with screen readers. These are internal working files that coordinators sort and filter, so keeping both is right.

Three gaps remain in the workbooks. First, the cuadro, puestos and horas workbooks do not state which roster version they hold (published on which date, or a draft), although the printed roster now does. Second, no workbook states the date rule for overnight hours. Amion's documentation warns that **an overnight shift counted toward the next day creates a phantom shift** in tally reports ([Amion](https://support.amion.com/hc/en-us/articles/26324867136275-Tally-Statistic-Reports)). The app already follows one consistent rule: `segmentos` in `an/turnos/recargos.py` splits surcharge hours at midnight, and each shift counts on the day it starts. The workbook should say so in one line, because a 19:00–07:00 Saturday night carrying seven hours of Sunday surcharge is exactly where payroll staff will ask. Third, the "Patrones" sheet still holds a weekday table, two lines of text and a Bradford table. That breaks the one-table-per-sheet rule and leaves only the first table filterable; splitting it into a weekday sheet and a Bradford sheet fixes both. Separately, `_terminar` hard-codes A4. If the service's printers load carta (US Letter), confirm that before release, or leave the paper size to the printer as the print CSS already does.

Payroll is the one Excel feature where vendor practice points the wrong way for this app. Deputy's custom payroll exporter lets users name and format each column, add header lines and value-rewrite rules, and share templates between managers. It also **fails if a field is empty or if the date range does not match the pay run** ([Deputy](https://whatsnew.deputy.com/new-custom-payroll-exporter-features-now-available-1Wi4Jq)). Planday maps a salary code to each shift type so sick hours land on their own lines ([Planday](https://help.planday.com/en/articles/4413251-payroll-prints)). Rotaready exports **signed-off data only** ([Rotaready](https://help.rotaready.com/en/articles/5799909-reports)). That much configuration suits a vendor serving thousands of employers on different payroll systems. One hospital has one payroll system. The Colombian evidence shows how specific the target will be: Senior/Novasoft's import of "novedades", which include overtime and surcharges, requires **unchanged column headers and a sheet named "DATOS"** ([Senior/Novasoft](https://documentacao.senior.com.br/es/novasoft/manual-del-usuario/docs/manuales_de_usuario/n%C3%B3mina/manuales/IMPORTACI%C3%93N%20DE%20NOVEDADES%20E%20HIST%C3%93RICO%20DE%20VACACIONES.pdf)). No templates turned up for Siigo or Alegra. The right move is to wait until the payroll system is named, then build one fixed template from closed periods only. The hours bank already follows that rule, and it matches Rotaready's signed-off approach.

Two other vendor features do not fit a portable app. UKG emails reports on a schedule as PDF, CSV or XLSX, and recommends CSV for large reports because Excel renders them slowly ([UKG via University of South Alabama](https://usa50.southalabama.edu/departments/financialaffairs/payroll/resources/dataviews-and-reports.pdf)). Humanity sends daily alerts about staff who are close to overtime ([TCP Humanity](https://tcpsoftware.com/products/humanity/reporting/)). Scheduled email would mean adding and securing outgoing mail, which the app does not have. A 40-person workbook is never slow, and the period zip already provides the monthly bundle. Sling-style report builders let users pick and reorder columns, show hours as h:mm or decimals, and choose totals or detail ([Sling](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Sling-by-Toast-Reports)). They solve a many-customer problem this app does not have.

## The printed roster became a control document; a daily sheet and change notices are next

Vendors treat the printout as a product in its own right, not a screenshot of the screen. Deputy prints **published shifts in green and unpublished ones in yellow**, puts the logo top-left, prints by area, week or day, and stacks two-week views one week under the other ([Deputy](https://www.deputy.com/blog/schedule-printing-improved)). When I Work has print toggles for annotations, availability, open shifts, positions, notes and unscheduled staff, and **watermarks unpublished schedules** ([When I Work](https://help.wheniwork.com/articles/printing-the-schedule-computer)). Infor's weekly printout shows absences and availability under each shift ([Infor](https://docs.infor.com/wfm/2026/en-us/wfmclolh/lfso_admin_cloud/fna1632159426511.html)). Connecteam lets users choose what to include before printing ([Connecteam](https://help.connecteam.com/en/articles/6453258-can-i-export-my-team-s-schedule)). The common thread is that a roster on the wall is a control document. Whoever reads it needs to know which version it is and who approved it.

`836b510` delivers that. The printed roster now opens with a header line naming the area, the period and the version: "Original publicada", "Publicado el …, con los cambios registrados" or **"BORRADOR: sin publicar"**. The header also shows the print date. The full legend, previously screen-only, now prints, and a "Revisó / Fecha / Firma" line closes the page. The layout follows the print checklist. The grid fills 100% of the page width with fixed table layout, a 30 mm name column, 7.5 pt type and 1 px padding. Sticky positioning is removed, the header row repeats on each page through `display: table-header-group`, and rows never split across pages. A4 landscape with 10 mm margins leaves **277 mm**, about **8 mm per day** in a 31-day month. Because `@page { size: landscape }` names no paper size, the same table also fits carta (about 259 mm usable, about 7.4 mm per day). One check remains: print a 31-day month to PDF from the browser the coordinators actually use, because DevTools print emulation does not show page breaks ([MDN @page size](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@page/size); [MDN print-color-adjust](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/print-color-adjust)).

Two print views are still missing. The first is a **daily sign-in sheet**. Vendors offer a "Daily Schedule Sign-In" report listing position, employee, shift time and hours, with **a space for the manager's signature** ([Inclusion Systems](https://inclusionsystems.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360045223972-Sign-Off-Sheets)). The app already has the day's assignments, the position assignment and the shift hours, so the view is one date, rows by position and a signature column. The second is **change notices**. The roster workbook already has an Original sheet and a Cambios sheet of differences, and Reportes already copies one person's schedule as text for chat. A "copy changes" action per affected person could list each changed date explicitly ("jueves 12/11: M → N"). That turns the diff into a notice for only the people affected, and gives coordinators the audit trail QGenda users say they lack. Print toggles in the style of When I Work, and font-size controls, can wait until a coordinator asks for them.

## Two flaws in the analytics code outrank any new chart

**Coverage nets surpluses against shortfalls across positions.** `cobertura_tramos` measures the gap in each slot before summing. That avoids Infor's warning that **surplus in one period can mask a shortfall in another** ([Infor](https://docs.infor.com/wfm/2026.10/en-us/wfmopolh/mvs_legacy_user_on-premise/dvu1475661991644.html)). But its docstring says it adds up all positions within each slot, so a spare person in one position cancels a missing person in another. That is the same netting, moved from time to position. The total is sound only if anyone can staff any position, and the app's per-position training records say they cannot. UKG's healthcare reports show coverage variance **per job** in 15-minute steps ([UKG](https://www.ukg.com/blogs/ukg-blog-post/make-better-healthcare-staffing-decisions-these-ukg-dimensions-reports)), while the app's per-position view counts days with an uncovered position, not hours. `836b510` adds coverage and efficiency per period, which helps, but these are still totals across positions. Computing missing and surplus staff-hours per position from the position assignment, and showing them as a monthly bar per position, fixes the measure without new data.

**The fatigue streak ignores shift length.** ANA sets a ceiling of **three consecutive 12-hour shifts** and 10–12 h of rest between shifts; NANN recommends at least 11 h of rest ([NANN](https://nann.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/The-Effect-of-Shift-Work-and-Fatigue_FINAL_2026-1.pdf)). The app's 12 h minimum-rest rule already exceeds that, and its limit of two nights in a row is stricter than ANA's. But the streak signal in `fatiga` defaults to more than **six days in a row**, whatever the shift length. That suits 8-hour shifts, not 12-hour ones. Counting consecutive 12-hour shifts separately, with a configurable default of three, takes a few lines. The source's publication date is unclear (2025 or 2026), and it is a nursing standard, so label it as a reference, not a rule.

The **rule-breach register** is half built. UKG assigns each breach a severity: informational, warning, or blocking the save ([UKG via University of Rochester](https://tech.rochester.edu/wp-content/uploads/Track_Rule_Violations.pdf)). Shiftboard audits manual overrides and records the justification ([Shiftboard](https://www.shiftboard.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/scheduling-compliance-solution-sheet-shiftboard.pdf)). `836b510`'s Alertas sheet covers the severity half. It lists alerts from `reglas.py` (five error rules, nine warning rules, one info rule) and from the position assignment, sorted by level and date. The justification half already exists in the change log, which stores an exception flag and a reason for every change. Adding a "reason for the exception" column, joined on person and date, completes the compliance record Planday sells as a breach report.

Two threshold checks reuse existing machinery. Humanity flags people heading into overtime by adding hours already worked to upcoming shifts ([TCP Humanity](https://tcpsoftware.com/products/humanity/reporting/)). In a fully assigned monthly roster, that projection is the roster itself. The useful form is therefore a warning-level rule in `reglas.py` for weeks above the **12 h a week overtime limit of CST art. 168** ([Gerencie](https://www.gerencie.com/horas-extras-y-recargos-nocturnos-dominicales-y-festivos.html)). A labour lawyer should confirm how that limit applies to 12-hour shift arrangements under the shift-work article. The second check is an hours-bank tolerance band. NHS e-rostering policy caps unused hours carried forward at one shift (7.5 h) ([Leicestershire Partnership NHS](https://www.leicspart.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Electronic-Rostering-Policy-Exp-Oct-28.pdf)), and NHS England targets under 13 h net hours per WTE ([NHS England](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/E-rostering_meaningful_use_standards.pdf)). Flagging people whose balance exceeds one shift (12 h here) in either direction, and reporting the share outside the band, adds that framing to the existing Banco sheet.

A **period scorecard** is worth building as separate traffic lights, not as one score. HotSchedules shows a colour-coded score while the schedule is built, with **flags that explain each issue** (demand vs scheduled hours, overtime risk, availability, labour rules, rest), and tracks the score over time ([Fourth/HotSchedules](https://www.fourth.com/solution/workforce-management-software/schedule-score)). Zebra proposes a weighted score built from under- and overstaffing, cost including premiums, rule compliance, swap frequency and skill match ([Zebra](https://zebra.com/us/en/blog/posts/2023/how-and-why--you-should-assess-schedule-quality-in-workforce-management-software.html)). The weighting is the weak part. Nurse-rostering experiments found that weighted sums produce the least fair rosters, because one person's bad outcome gets offset by others' good ones ([Martin et al.](https://people.cs.nott.ac.uk/pszeo/docs/publications/fairnessNurseRosteringCoop.pdf)). What makes HotSchedules useful is the explanatory flags, and they survive without the weighting. One row per period would show coverage %, breaches by severity, fairness deviation, changes after publication and lead time. Each figure gets a configurable green/amber/red threshold and a trend. The row does the job of a score while keeping every number explainable to staff.

Three further metrics need data or judgement the app lacks. A **workload ratio** like hours per patient day is how Infor and the NHS normalise staffing. Infor reports target vs actual hours per patient day, with agency and non-productive hours ([Infor](https://docs.infor.com/wfm/7.1.0/en-us/wfmolh/wfmprug/yfw1475662031368.html)). NHS CHPPD splits planned and actual hours by staff group ([NHS England](https://england.nhs.uk/long-read/care-hours-per-patient-day-chppd-guidance-for-all-inpatient-trusts)). The local equivalent, staff-hours per 100 instrument sets or per imaging study, needs monthly production counts; ask the service for them rather than fake the figure. **Turnover warning signs** can partly be computed today. Examples include sick calls rising against the person's own history, preferences repeatedly overridden or no longer submitted, extra shifts no longer picked up, and overtime creeping up ([M7 Health](https://www.m7health.com/blogs/scheduling-data-nurse-turnover-signals)). But the source is a vendor blog and the signals target individuals. If built, this should be a coordinator-only check-in list, carrying the same "not for sanctions" label as the Bradford sheet. **Budget vs planned vs actual**, offered by Deputy, Humanity and Sling ([Deputy](https://help.deputy.com/hc/en-au/articles/4764591177871)), needs a budget the app does not hold. An hours-only version would mostly duplicate the contracted vs scheduled view the hours export already gives.

One vendor metric should stay out entirely. MangoApps' per-person reliability score runs from 0 to 1 and is built from weighted parts with tier thresholds ([MangoApps](https://www.mangoapps.com/help/workforce_intelligence)). Here it would rank 40 named colleagues on one opaque number, invite punitive use, and swing on one or two events. Two other common metrics need no new work. ShiftMed's fill rate and time-to-fill for open shifts ([ShiftMed](https://www.shiftmed.com/insights/knowledge-center/healthcare-workforce-metrics/)) are covered by the replacement rate PR 70 added. RLDatix-style publication lead time, where one case used a 42-day target ([RLDatix](https://www.rldatix.com/en-uki/solution/workforce-insights/)), already appears in the stability sheet as days of notice at publication. The research notes also flag radiology credential expiry and dosimetry records as a later item, but they captured no source for the regulations, so check the current rules before scoping it.

## Spacing is settled: one density, sized by pointer, with paper as the exception

The major design systems agree on a 4 px base, an 8 px rhythm and a small set of steps; they differ mainly in naming. The app's primitive steps (0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 40, 48, 64 px) fit inside every scale in the table below. PR 71 enforces them: `checks/diseno.py` now rejects arbitrary spacing values outside the roster grid, and the shared `Seccion` and `Vacio` components carry the section rhythm.

| System | Scale (px) | Table rows (px) | Card / container padding (px) |
|---|---|---|---|
| IBM Carbon | 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48, 64, 80, 96, 160 | 24 / 32 / **40 default** / 48 / 64 | 16 in grid boxes |
| Primer | 2–128, in 4 px steps up to 48 | about 28 / 36 / 44 (12 px text, 20 px line-height) | stacks of 8 / 16 / 24 |
| Atlassian | 0–80 | steps of 0–8 for table cells | 12–24 inside cards |
| Cloudscape | 2–40 | n/a | 16 in cards, 20 on container sides |
| Material | 4 dp per density step | 52 dp default | n/a |
| An today | 4 px scale (PR 71) | header 36, **rows 40**; roster cells about 32, 44 on touch | 16 on mobile, 20 from `sm` |

Sources: [Carbon spacing](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/spacing/overview/), [Carbon data table](https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/data-table/style/), [Primer size](https://primer.style/foundations/primitives/size), [Primer DataTable CSS](https://github.com/primer/react/blob/main/packages/react/src/DataTable/Table.module.css), [Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/spacing), [Cloudscape](https://cloudscape.design/foundation/visual-foundation/spacing/), [Material density](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@material/density@14.0.0/README.md).

The app already makes the choices the evidence favours. Every data table goes through `Tabla` at the Untitled UI `sm` size, which gives **40 px rows**, the Carbon default. Material's 52 dp default would make a 40-person list 30% taller. The roster grid uses `pointer-coarse:h-11`, so cells grow to 44 px on touch screens, the same switch Primer makes between `pointer: coarse` and `pointer: fine`. Under WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8, the clickable target is the cell of about 30 × 32 px, not the 22 px chip inside it, so the grid meets the **24 × 24 px minimum** ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). Cloudscape warns that compact density hurts readability and target size, so it **should never replace comfortable**. It also says density should be one saved app-wide setting, never mixed within a page ([Cloudscape](https://cloudscape.design/foundation/visual-foundation/content-density/)). The app already has one density with pointer-based sizing, so a toggle would add a setting nobody has asked for. If one is ever needed, Tailwind v4 makes it a block of variable overrides under `[data-density="compact"]` plus a `@custom-variant` ([Tailwind](https://tailwindcss.com/docs/adding-custom-styles)).

Paper is the one place that needs its own density, and `836b510` sets it in the print stylesheet only: 1 px cell padding, 7.5 pt type and a 30 mm name column, with the screen tokens untouched. One cosmetic mismatch remains. Carbon makes the header row the same height as body rows ([Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/data-table/style/)), but the app's tables put 36 px headers over 40 px rows. Fixing it is a one-class change, worth making only the next time the table component is touched. Everything else should keep passing the proximity test: space inside a group must be clearly smaller than space between groups, and a heading must sit closer to its own section than to the one above ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gestalt-proximity/)).

Each comes from nursing, NHS or secondary legal sources, not from Colombian sterilisation or radiology practice.

## Conclusion

The question for the app has shifted from "does it compute the right thing" to "can someone who never opens it trust what it hands them". `836b510` answers most of the second question by exposing context the app already held: the version, the legend, the alerts and the generation time. Most of what remains works the same way. The exception reasons are in the change log, the change notices are in the existing diff, and the midnight rule is in the surcharge code. Checking the research against the code turned up something more important than a missing feature. Two measures are quietly wrong for this service: coverage nets positions against each other, and the fatigue streak is sized for 8-hour shifts. Neither shows up in a vendor comparison, and both should be fixed before more charts are added.

The vendor survey also shows what not to copy. Configurable payroll exporters, report builders, scheduled email and density toggles exist to serve many customers with different needs. A single-service portable app has one payroll system, one team and a handful of coordinators, so the right version of each is a fixed one. The only new input worth asking the service for is monthly production counts. With them, the app could judge staffing against actual workload rather than against demand settings the coordinators typed in themselves.
